# PRD Quality Review — Toppsvar PRD

Reviewed: `.docs/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-Toppsvar-2026-10-12/prd.md` (status `draft`, no PRD addendum). Stakes: graded course project, chain-top (feeds UX, architecture, epics/stories built by AI agents). Calibrated for testability, a stable core flow, and an examiner running the app without the group's keys.

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, unusually honest draft: the thesis is sharp (the Judge replaces the one step that needed a person), scope is cut into a core flow and a deferred Stage 2, anonymity trade-offs are named rather than smoothed, and nearly every FR carries a testable consequence. What is at risk is the anonymity mechanics, which are the product's headline promise: FR-10, FR-12, FR-3 and UJ-2 contradict each other on what a link does after close; the Collection-time interface guarantee has no testable FR; and the Invite link lifecycle (how the Host obtains and identifies links) is unspecified. A second gap is that nothing says where the app runs for real Participants, while NFR-1 forbids group-owned hosting. There are no critical findings, but four high ones should be resolved before UX and architecture start; the 22 [ASSUMPTION] tags are an acceptable density for a draft (they round-trip cleanly), and most are low-risk numeric parameters.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions, and trade-offs name what was given up: FR-8 (count only, at the cost of progress visibility), FR-9 (keep a non-empty set at the cost of possible double counting), FR-10 (minimum of three Answer sets), the Stage 2 deferral of Question sets in §8.2 with a real `[NOTE FOR PM]`. Open Questions 1, 2, 3 and 5 are real. Open Question 3 half-answers itself ("Consider showing the count only once it reaches three") but the tension is genuine.

Where it falls short: the PRD never decides where the product runs for real Participants (SM-5 pilots and UJ-1/UJ-2 need phones reaching a public URL across days, NFR-1 says "no hosted service owned by the group"). It also leaves the Stage 1 "no discard of a Group" consequence unsurfaced although the PRD itself says answers name non-consenting people and nothing is moderated (§6.1, §6.3). A decision-maker pushing back on either would find the objection dodged, not acknowledged.

### Findings
- **[high]** No deployment/reachability requirement; NFR-1 appears to forbid it (§5 NFR-1, UJ-1, UJ-2, SM-5) — NFR-1 requires "no hosted service owned by the group", yet Participants must open Invite links on their phones over several days, and SM-5 has outside Hosts running real gatherings. A localhost-only app cannot serve that flow. The PRD is silent on how the real flow is reachable (hosted instance, tunnel, or "examiner-local only"). *Fix:* split NFR-1 into (a) examiner runnability from a clone with no keys and no accounts, and (b) a pilot/real-use requirement that Participant phones can reach the application at a URL, leaving the mechanism to architecture. State explicitly if the pilot is out of semester scope.
- **[medium]** Stage 1 has no way to remove a Group from the TV (FR-21 is Stage 2; §6.3 says no moderation; §6.1 says Answers name non-consenting people) — a hurtful Group in the top eight goes on a shared screen; the only workaround is renaming it to something innocuous. This is a real trade-off that is not surfaced. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` at FR-21 or FR-20, or promote "discard" to Stage 1 (it is one flag on a Group).
- **[low]** Open Question 3 contains its own recommendation (§10 Q3) — fine, but state which is the current default so downstream does not guess. *Fix:* "Default until decided: FR-8 and FR-10 as written."

## Substance over theater — strong

The Vision is specific (the housewarming origin, the Judge as the one automated step) and could not be swapped into another PRD. NFRs carry thresholds (360 px, 1920×1080, one second, WCAG 2.1 AA, three setup commands). Four journey protagonists, each driving requirements: Maren drives FR-4 to FR-10 and FR-17 to FR-20, Jonas FR-6/FR-7, Siri FR-29/FR-30, Eirik FR-31. Non-Users in §2.2 are real exclusions. The only furniture is mild.

### Findings
- **[low]** The Group-social and Host-social jobs (§2.1: "look organised doing it", "laugh at ourselves") drive no requirement. *Fix:* cut them, or tie each to an FR.
- **[low]** SM-C3 (§9) is a constraint, not a metric. *Fix:* move to §6 as a guardrail or keep and accept it as a design rule.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is stated and the prioritisation follows from it: stabilise write → collect → group → review → play before anything else (§1, §8). Stage 2 order is justified (ProtoQA sets first). Counter-metrics exist (SM-C1 to SM-C3). The weakness is that the metrics only partly validate the thesis. The bet is "a host can correct the grouping in minutes", but no metric measures minutes, and SM-2 is gameable.

### Findings
- **[medium]** SM-2 is maximised by not grouping (§9 SM-2) — "at least 70 % of Answers land in a Group whose majority belongs to the same hand-made cluster" is a purity measure; one Group per Answer scores 100 %. SM-C1 counterbalances SM-4, not SM-2. It also measures English ProtoQA while the product is Norwegian (NFR-6, Open Question 1), so it says little about Norwegian slang. *Fix:* use pairwise precision and recall (or F1) against the reference clusters, state its relation to the Norwegian caveat, and make SM-C2 or a new counter-metric cover over-splitting.
- **[medium]** SM-4 and SM-C2 need data no FR produces (§9 SM-4, SM-C2) — "median corrected Group needs one edit" and "Judge calls per Game" require logging of Host edits and Judge calls, but no FR or NFR requires that, and FR-11 and §6.1 push against logging. *Fix:* add an FR or NFR for a local, content-free counter of edits and Judge calls per Game, or mark SM-4 as measured by hand in pilots only.
- **[low]** No metric for the stated "minutes" (§1) — add "Review of a 9-Question Game completes within N minutes" (UJ-3 implies about twenty).
- **[low]** SM-1 claims every testable consequence is covered by an automated test (§9 SM-1), but FR-23 "legible at 3 metres" and parts of NFR-5 cannot be automated. *Fix:* say "automated or manually checked per a stated checklist".

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs have concrete consequences with bounds (20 Questions, 200 characters, 50 links, 80 characters, 40-character labels, eight Groups, 2 to 4 Teams, two-minute Grouping). FR-12 includes an automated schema-inspection test, which is exactly the kind of statement story creation can lean on. The weak points are contradictions between FRs, missing FRs for behaviour the prose promises, and a handful of undefined states and adjectives.

### Findings
- **[high]** Closed versus unknown links cannot both hold (§4.3 FR-10, §4.1 FR-3, §4.4 FR-12, UJ-2 Resolution, Glossary Token) — FR-10 says after close "every Invite link of the Game shows that Collection is closed", UJ-2 says "the link shows that collection is closed", and FR-3 says a deleted Game's links respond "as unknown links, not as closed ones". But FR-12 says after close "no stored table holds a Token, a Token hash", and the Glossary says the Token "is discarded when Collection closes". With no stored token material, the system cannot tell a closed link from a never-existed one, nor refuse a submission "even if the form was opened before" (FR-10). *Fix:* state the intended mechanism at requirement level, for example that the Token is self-describing (embeds the Game identity) so a closed Game can be recognised without a stored per-link record, and rewrite FR-12 to "no stored association between a Token and an Answer set" with a testable list of what may remain; or drop the "shows closed" requirement and make closed and unknown links respond identically. Update UJ-2 and the Glossary accordingly.
- **[high]** No testable consequence for the interface-side anonymity promise (§4.4 description, FR-8, FR-11, FR-12, SM-3) — §4.4 says nothing links an Invite link to its Answers "during Collection through the interface", but FR-11 and FR-12 only cover the answering flow and post-close storage. Nothing says the Host cannot read Answers before close, that no page lists per-link state, or that Review never exposes which Answers share an Answer set (which would make a person's whole submission reconstructible, defeating FR-12). *Fix:* add consequences: no Host page or export shows Answer text or Answer sets before close; no Host page shows any per-Invite-link submitted or not-submitted state; Review shows Groups and Answers but no Answer-set identity; add these to the SM-3 audit.
- **[high]** Invite link lifecycle is underspecified (§4.3 FR-5, FR-9, Glossary Token, UJ-1 Path and Edge case) — the Glossary says the Token "is never shown to the Host as data", yet the Host pastes each link into a message (UJ-1). It is unstated whether links can be re-copied after creation (impossible if only a one-way hash is stored, FR-12), how the Host identifies the one link to revoke for a friend who lost theirs (FR-9) without a label (which would link links to people), and what the Host's link list shows. FR-9 is not implementable or testable until this is decided. *Fix:* add a consequence on display: links are shown once at creation and cannot be retrieved, or are listed by ordinal with a Host-visible copy action; state how the Host picks the link to revoke; state in FR-8 whether revoked links count in the denominator.
- **[medium]** "Reviewed" is undefined, and Review's display has no FR (§4.6 description, FR-20) — the description says the Host "sees the Groups, their Counts and the raw Answers in each", but FR-17 to FR-20 are only operations. FR-20 requires "every Question has been reviewed" with no action or state that marks a Question reviewed, and nothing says whether Review edits persist across sessions (UJ-3 "twenty minutes before leaving"). *Fix:* add an FR for the Review view (Groups, Counts, raw Answers per Group, ranked) and a per-Question "mark reviewed" consequence; state that edits are saved as they are made.
- **[medium]** "Open Collection" is implicit (§4.2 FR-4, §4.3 FR-5) — FR-4 speaks of Collection "opening" and refuses it with zero Questions, but the only trigger is FR-5 "creating Invite links moves the Game from *draft* to *collecting*". The zero-Questions refusal therefore belongs on FR-5 and is currently misplaced. *Fix:* state that creating the first Invite link opens Collection and put the zero-Question refusal there, or add an explicit "Open Collection" action.
- **[medium]** FR-1 has no testable consequence for the account itself (§4.1 FR-1) — "create an account, log in and log out" lists no credentials, no minimum password rule, no failure behaviour, no session expiry; the only consequences are access control. "Any other request is refused" does not say how. *Fix:* add consequences for wrong credentials, duplicate account, logout invalidating the session, and password storage (never plain text, ties to NFR-7).
- **[medium]** Adjectives and undefined bounds (FR-5, FR-16, FR-23) — "cannot feasibly be guessed" (FR-5) has no bound; "Judge fails or times out" (FR-16) names no timeout; "legible at 3 metres" (FR-23) has no font size. *Fix:* "at least 128 bits of randomness", a timeout value (for example 30 seconds per call, a stated assumption), and a minimum text size or a checklist item.
- **[medium]** FR-16 contradicts itself on the fallback grouping (§4.5 FR-16, Glossary Grouping) — "each Answer starts as its own Group" versus "one Group per distinct Answer text". Whether "Pizza" and "pizza " are the same text is also unstated (normalisation of case, whitespace and punctuation), and it changes Counts. *Fix:* choose one rule and state the normalisation, in FR-16 and in FR-14's Judge-free path.
- **[medium]** FR-30 and UJ-5 disagree on Demo mode (§4.8 FR-30, UJ-5 Entry state and Path) — the demo Game "is already collected and closed" with stored groupings, but FR-30 tests "closing Collection on the demo Game produces Judge-proposed Groups from the stored rulings", and FR-14 says Grouping runs once at close. Also unspecified: what happens in Demo mode when the examiner creates their own Game (stored rulings do not cover it, so FR-16 fallback is the only path). *Fix:* decide whether the demo Game ships closed-and-grouped or collecting-and-ready-to-close, and say what Demo mode does for Answers it has no ruling for.
- **[medium]** FR-12's "no order of arrival" and Count are in tension (§4.4 FR-12, Glossary Count) — Count is "distinct Answer sets", so an Answer-set identity must survive close; the PRD never says so, and "stored in an order that reveals nothing" is not directly testable (row order, auto-increment keys). *Fix:* state that a random, non-sequential Answer-set identifier may remain, forbid sequential identifiers and timestamps, and say Answers are shuffled at close so the test can check it.
- **[medium]** Dead end and undefined "submitted" at close (§4.3 FR-6, FR-7, FR-10) — if fewer than three Answer sets exist, the Host cannot close, with no stated recourse. "Submitted Answer set" is undefined when Answers can be removed (FR-7) or when only some Questions were answered (FR-6 requires at least one Answer per Question only in an assumption, not a consequence). *Fix:* define a submitted Answer set (at least one Answer, or all Questions answered), say what a set with all Answers removed counts as, and state the Host's options when fewer than three exist.
- **[medium]** No state-transition table and no persistence requirement for play (Glossary Game, FR-10, FR-14, FR-20, FR-26) — states are scattered across FRs, there is no "grouping in progress" state although Grouping can take up to two minutes after close, and nothing says whether play state (Reveals, Scores, current Team) survives a browser reload on the shared screen or whether a *played* Game can be replayed. A party game that loses its Score on refresh is a core-flow failure. *Fix:* add a one-table state machine (state, allowed actions, entry action) and a consequence that play state is persisted on every Reveal and survives reload.
- **[low]** FR-24 asks the Host to type a Guess that Stage 1 never uses (§4.7 FR-24) — nothing displays the Guess or feeds it anywhere; the Host just clicks a Group. Typing it is friction during live play. *Fix:* show the Guess on the shared screen with the Reveal, or drop the typing from Stage 1 and keep it for FR-27.
- **[low]** FR-25 undo versus unscored Reveals (FR-25, FR-26) — "undo the most recent Reveal" is ambiguous when the last Reveal was an unscored one. *Fix:* say whether it covers both and what it restores.
- **[low]** FR-28 has no consequences (§4.7) — acceptable for a Stage 2 item flagged `[NOTE FOR PM]`, noted only for completeness.
- **[low]** Small gaps: FR-2 gives no limit on the Game title; FR-17 does not break a tie when choosing the merged label; FR-15's default-label rule (shortest text on ties) is an inference left untagged.

## Scope honesty — strong

§7 does real work (no Participant accounts, no payments, no unconfirmed Board, no remote play) and each Stage 2 item is deferred openly, not silently. 22 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, 2 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, 5 Open Questions: for a draft awaiting group confirmation and a project whose core flow still has to be built, the density is acceptable. Most assumptions are numeric parameters that can be confirmed in one sitting; the risky ones are FR-8, FR-9, FR-10, FR-4 locking and NFR-6. `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit on deferred items only; the live tensions (FR-8 and FR-10) are covered by Open Question 3 instead, which is acceptable.

### Findings
- **[low]** Triage the assumptions before confirmation: separate numeric parameters (limits, thresholds) from behavioural decisions (FR-4 locking, FR-8, FR-9, FR-10, FR-26) so the group spends its time on the second kind.
- **[low]** §6.2 refers to "the largest Game in FR-14" (30 sets, 15 Questions) while FR-5 and FR-4 permit 50 links and 20 Questions; the cost bound is ambiguous. *Fix:* name the sizing Game explicitly.

## Downstream usability — adequate

IDs are contiguous and unique (FR-1 to FR-31, NFR-1 to NFR-7, SM-1 to SM-6, SM-C1 to SM-C3, UJ-1 to UJ-6, Open Questions 1 to 5), cross-references resolve, every UJ has a named protagonist, and each feature section names the UJ it realises. Sections read acceptably alone. The weak point is Glossary discipline.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The UJs and several FRs do not use the Glossary terms verbatim (UJ-1 to UJ-6, §4.7, FR-31) — in UJ-1 to UJ-4 the domain nouns are lowercase and sometimes synonyms: "questions", "answers", "groups", "invite links", "collection", "review", "board", "teams", "score". The Glossary has no "top-list", yet UJ-1, UJ-3 and the Vision use it where "Group" or "Board" is meant ("nine top-lists already grouped", "a ranked top-list she agrees with"). Undefined terms in use: **play mode** (UJ-3, UJ-4, FR-31) and **play view** (FR-23, NFR-3) alongside an undefined "Play"; **answer form** (FR-6, FR-13, NFR-3); **Game page** (UJ-1); **cluster** (SM-2, FR-31) used as a Group synonym for the dataset's groups; **Judge rulings** (Glossary Demo mode, FR-30). *Fix:* capitalise Glossary terms in UJs and FRs and replace "top-list" with "Group" or "Board"; add Glossary entries for Play (with *play mode* as the only name, retire "play view") and Answer form, or replace them with existing terms; either map "cluster" to Group in FR-31 or define it as a dataset term.
- **[medium]** The Reveal and Score definitions conflict with FR-26 (Glossary Reveal, Score; FR-25, FR-26) — Reveal is "Showing a hidden Group ... after a Guess matches it", but FR-26 reveals Groups "without a match". Score is "the sum of the Counts of the Groups a Team has revealed", but an unscored Reveal belongs to no Team. *Fix:* define Reveal as showing a hidden Group, and Score as the sum of the Counts of Groups *awarded to* a Team; update FR-25's invariant to match.
- **[low]** UJ-2 and UJ-4 have no edge case, and UJ-6 is compressed to one line without Entry state or Path; fine for Stage 2, but a UJ for the Judge failing partway (FR-16) and for a lost-and-revoked link used by the old holder would exercise FR-9 and FR-16.

## Shape fit — strong

Consumer party product feeding a chain, so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing and are present; the examiner journey (UJ-5) is an apt addition for this assessment context. Not over-formalised; Stage tags on every FR make epic cutting straightforward. The Host-only account model and no-accounts Participants are consistent in the Glossary and FRs.

### Findings
- None beyond the Glossary and Done-ness items above.
